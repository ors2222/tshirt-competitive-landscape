# Design concepts for review

These are text-first concepts, not user-tested wireframes, measurements, or final brand decisions. Product facts must remain the same across a comparison so that feedback is about the presentation rather than a changed offer.

## Concept A — The Clear Basic

**Feeling:** calm, direct, easy to trust.

**Page idea:** one real shirt photo, clear size guide, known fabric and care facts, price, and one obvious size action. A small “make it yours” link leads to an optional idea guide.

**Strength:** helps visitors understand what they are buying. Fits a low-friction basic-retail path.

**Risk:** may look interchangeable with a generic blank unless real customer needs support a stronger angle.

## Concept B — The Creative Guide

**Feeling:** helpful, curious, hands-on.

**Page idea:** show the shirt clearly first, then offer an honest, short guide to ways a blank can be personalized. Separate verified methods from methods still being tested. State whether supplies or printing are included.

**Strength:** creates useful content around the creative audience hypothesis without claiming to sell custom printing.

**Risk:** if paint or marker performance is unverified, the story can overpromise. A guide also adds work the team must maintain.

## Concept C — The Point of View

**Feeling:** expressive, art-directed, confident.

**Page idea:** use the postmodernist-leaning hero direction already selected in Brand Studio, with the shirt as the visual anchor and minimal copy that explains the product and the buyer's creative role.

**Strength:** gives the basic a memorable point of view and builds on the learner's chosen visual direction.

**Risk:** the existing review notes say styling should support the shirt, not overshadow it. Wordmark, palette, and audience effects are still unreviewed or hypothetical.

## Review prompts

Use the same questions for each concept:

1. What do you think is being sold, and what do you expect to receive?
2. What would you want to know before choosing a size?
3. Does the page suggest that supplies, printing, or a paint-ready finish are included? What led you to that reading?
4. What would you do next, and what information is missing?

Record actual answers without rewriting them as proof of demand. See [the opportunity plan](/opportunities) for a lightweight validation sequence.
